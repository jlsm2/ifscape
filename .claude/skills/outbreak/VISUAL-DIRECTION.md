# 2D visual direction (apply from Part 4 onward)

Source: the owner's audit of Part 3 and the 2D animation visual-direction skill (local audit notes). Read it when making the beats (step 2) and when checking the sheet (step 6). **The approved style does not change** (figure, outline, amber/Vacuum palette, typography, economy of movement). Part 3 itself stays frozen until the owner says otherwise.

## Part 3 diagnosis (what led to the rule)
- Still image while the line advances: scene 03 (empty alley, only a zoom) and scenes 05 to 07 (Sam in profile in the same place, same framing, ~15 s). The text carried the progression.
- Dialogue scene (02) frontal and repeated: the lines change, the pose does not.
- The set read as a wall with glued-on objects; no foreground.
- What worked and repeats: the shadow at the back of the alley mouth, Sam contained in the foreground, the distance between the two as the focus, stillness with intent.

## Rules (they add to the story rules in SKILL.md)
1. **Beats, not words.** Group the line into dramatic beats; do not change the image at every caption. Cut when information, focus, action, emotional scale or spatial orientation changes.
2. **Every shot evolves.** Avoid the same composition for more than ~5 s without identifiable internal progression (heuristic: a significant change every 3 to 5 s in an expository stretch). A suspense hold only with written intent ("what the audience watches or expects"). Question before prolonging: does the image give new information, deliberately reinforce something or create anticipation?
3. **Directed stillness.** Still is not forgotten: during the wait, ONE thing changes (eyes before the body, light, a growing shadow, occlusion, an almost imperceptible push-in). Never animate everything; 1 or 2 signals per beat.
4. **Progressive reveal in every video**, not only in the last seconds: presence as a possibility (dark zone) → clue (shadow, sound) → perception (Sam looks) → partial reveal → hold. Light source and shadow direction established BEFORE the threat; coherent scale and footing. Do not show the whole infected before the script does.
5. **Develop the set before changing the set.** Same place, another framing: establishing (location) → medium (relationship, action) → close (decision, fear) → detail (the object that matters, e.g. truck marks, the pallet) → profile (distance) → silhouette/occlusion. Only detail supported by the line (no smoke, paper, rat, car).
6. **Depth by layers, not by excess:** foreground (door frame, wall edge, cropped box, shadow), action plane, background (door, pole, window, silhouette). In an environment, at least 2 legible layers; 3 when it fits. Separate by overlap, scale, contrast, ground shadow, subtle 2D parallax only on camera movement.
7. **Every movement has a cause** (show action, reaction, reveal, guide the eye, environmental condition). Without function, remove it.
8. **Blocking with micro-animation:** gaze, head tilt, weight, shoulders, hands, hesitation, clear start and end. In dialogue, 3 to 4 reactions (observes, holds, looks back, refuses) and vary the framing (duo → slight approach on the refusal → return), without cutting at every caption.
9. **Geography and screen direction.** Before running, record for each set: entrances and exits, immutable props (pallet, dumpster, door), light source, start and end position of each character, where they look. Sam on the left looking right does not flip without reason. A new set gets its sheet in `BIBLE.md`.
10. **Caption and image cooperate:** the caption does not cover eyes, gesture, threat or the mentioned object (scene 03 of Part 3: "left a pallet" over the set); `caption_y` chosen by the framing.
11. **Negative space with function** (atmosphere, text or expectation). In 9:16 an empty top/bottom band without function = reframe (zoom, `focus`) instead of leaving dark leftover.
12. **The image adds, does not repeat** the line (e.g. line says "the street is quiet" → a wide shot where the absence is unsettling, not a frame with people passing).
13. **End card as consequence**: the palette and the suspense rhythm hold for an instant before the CONTINUE card (alley sound drops, boom); no new image behind the text.

## Extra deliverable in step 2 (beats): short shot list
For each scene, one line with: time, dramatic beat, what the audience should notice / should NOT notice yet, framing (shot, zoom, focus) and what changes during the shot, foreground / action / background, final gaze and pose, light and shadow, sync with sound, where the caption sits, and "why it deserves this time" (scene > ~6 s without a response = restructure: split into 2 framings of the same set or give it an event). A field that does not apply: "not applicable".

## Beat map → what the 2D system already does (use it) and what is missing (ask for it or work around it)
- Already exists: camera `zoom` and `focus` per scene with a slow push-in (`camera: {zoom: [a,b], focus: [p,q]}`), `heat` (figures at the alley mouth, entering over 3 s), `recoil`, `expr`, `walk`, `talk`, `light`, `silhouette`/`appear` (close and duo), `closing`, set per scene (`wide`/`close`/`duo`).
- Missing and worth building when the episode calls for it (system work, `tools/flat2d/`): look/turn-the-head gesture (`look`), a scene with 2 framings of the same set without cutting the audio (split the line into two scenes with `min_seconds`), foreground as an RGBA layer (`front`) in new sets (the `alley` has none), detail/`insert` of props (tire marks, pallet), a growing shadow, subtle parallax. Outside the system, the rule: never fake it; cut the line or switch to a close-up.

## Mini-audit (run on the clip sheet, step 6, and note in `build/<slug>/review-visual.md`)
1. What changes visually per 5 s stretch, and why does it matter? 2. What information does the set give? 3. Is the distance between character, object and background clear? 4. Is any action required by the line missing? 5. Movement without cause to remove? 6. Would staging or light solve it without changing the set? 7. Does the reveal land at the right moment? 8. Does the shot deserve the time? 9. Is there internal variety even in the same place? 10. Was the approved style preserved?

## Approval checklist (the same list, short)
Action tied to the beat · consistent geography and screen direction · legible layers · the set informs · every movement has a cause · long pauses have intent · no stagnation or agitation · caption does not cover a clue · light obeys the source · new elements supported · same style · works in 9:16.

## Part 4 audit (local audit notes; apply from Part 5 onward)
Diagnosis: identity preserved; what failed was that the **image did not prove what the line says** and shots that do not evolve. Scores from the audit: depth 4/10, animation 4/10, text–image coherence 5/10.
14. **Text does not replace action (inventory and visual math).** "Two jugs" = two VISIBLE jugs and the act of taking them (anticipation → grab → lift → adjust to the weight). The profile `carry` hides the back hand's jug behind the torso: in Part 5, draw one in front and another offset/legible, or write "a jug". The HUD number only appears AFTER the action that justifies it, and the origin of each portion (the 16 L stored) is read on screen, not guessed.
15. **Spatial map per cut.** Before running, a table: where each one is, where they look, which hand holds what, which side the door is on and where it opens. Each cut is checked against the table; do not swap sides for composition. Door action line: notices → turns → step → hand on the door → goes through → whoever stays outside reaches out → the door reacts.
16. **A knock with visible cause and reaction.** 2 or 3 distinct hand contacts; the door vibrates slightly on impact (and the latch/plate); Sam reacts progressively (eyes before the body, shoulders harden). An effect with a cause, never decorative shaking.
17. **Absence = one concrete signal in the existing set.** Rosa missing: ONE object planted beforehand (chair pulled out, personal item, drawer ajar) plus the sustained empty space; the camera slowly advances to the void. No flashback, no other set.
18. **Reuse the set through dramatic transformation** (collection → threat → absence): change attention, scale, light or framing of the same place, do not swap the background to disguise a lack of staging. The pharmacy interior has 3 planes (foreground: shelf/box edge, action: Sam and counter, background: lower-contrast shelves); the transition through the door is shown (frame) or motivated.
19. **Recognition beat at the start** of the episode (Sam interrupts the movement, eyes before head, mouth closes) before revealing the threat. The `look` gesture is missing in the system (see map above).
20. **Watermark** (`@ifscape_lab`) stays in the safe band and consistent; in a close-up it never crosses face, hand, object or caption (check on the MP4; the Part 4 close-up had the mark over the body).
21. **Two-mode review** in step 8: with audio/caption (rhythm) and WITHOUT audio and without reading the text (are the basic actions still understood? what only exists in the line becomes visual reinforcement or a cut of the line).
Delivery per sequence (step 2): beat sheet + spatial map + list of actions with physical contact + depth plan + continuity checklist; do not declare done with a divergence of object, position, direction, quantity or cause/consequence.

## Part 4 revision audit (local audit notes; apply from Part 5 onward)
Diagnosis of v2: the direction is right, but the evolution is still timid. v2 added objects, repositioned characters and inserted close-ups, and that is not enough. **Style, detail and movement complexity do not change**; what is missing is true spatial depth, events distributed throughout the line and rigorous continuity between action, object and on-screen indicator.
22. **Resolution gate (mandatory before calling a scene resolved).** A new object, a repositioned character and an inserted close-up resolve NOTHING by themselves. For each fix, write the answer to the three questions in `review-visual.md`: (a) what new information or emotion does this change deliver to the viewer? (b) can it be understood in 5 s of video without audio and without reading the caption? (c) does the place still look like a real place in the style's logic (scale, ground support, overlap, light, the object is where the body is facing)? A "no" on (a) or (b) = the fix is decoration; remove or redo it. On (c) = a new defect (e.g. Part 4 v2, jugs set down behind Sam, who was looking the other way).
23. **True depth, not decorative.** Deliberate layers: foreground cut by the edge (shelf, box, door frame), action plane, intermediate plane, lower-contrast background; plus contact shadow and overlap. Test: mentally remove the foreground; if the reading of the space does not change, it is ornament. The foreground never covers action, caption or the mentioned object, and must READ as real furniture (vertical, with top and base resolved), never as a strip or cutout: the 1st attempt in Part 4 v2 (a slanted dark strip on the right edge of the pharmacy) came out looking like a render defect and was REMOVED; without a good foreground, stay with fog (`depth`) and contact shadow. A new set gets its own as an RGBA layer drawn into the set, not as a loose polygon.
24. **Distributed events.** In every scene > ~5 s, write the timeline of beats (every 3 to 5 s the gaze, action, framing, light, object or information changes) before rendering. A stretch where the image barely evolves (Part 4: ~42 to 50 s at the counter and ~62 to 72 s at the end) needs a beat with a cause (notices → hesitates → decides) or a written justification of dramatic immobility. Still by oversight is a defect.
25. **Continuity ledger (a table per shot, filled in at step 2 and checked at 6 and 8):** characters (position, where they look and walk, state), objects (quantity, size, holding hand, side where they land), doors (state, where they open, which side each one is on), light (source and direction) and set (landmarks). **HUD/chip:** the number on screen is the story's state at that instant; show the value BEFORE the action, the action, and only then the NEW value (Part 4: `16 L` → picks up the jugs → `26 L`); never a new value before the act nor an old one after.
26. **3-pass protocol with a log.** (1) Without audio and without caption: are the action and geography understood? does something appear or vanish without cause? (2) With voice and sound: sync of line, effect and reaction. (3) Continuity between shots (ledger). Log each problem in `review-visual.md` with: timecode, severity (CRITICAL/HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW), observable evidence, consequence for comprehension or emotion, minimal fix and dependencies on other shots. Do not declare the scene done because it looks nice or is more animated; only when clear, coherent and aligned with the narrative intent, with CRITICAL and HIGH fixed or justified.

## Season redo (Parts 1 to 4 approved after the audit of the final MP4s; apply from Part 5)
27. **A clue object never "spawns".** Every object that becomes proof (the clue to the absence, the item that pays off a line) has already been on screen, in the same place, in earlier scenes, and does not enter in the middle of a shot. Plant it in the first scene where the place appears (`flat.props` in the pharmacy wide; `pills` = Rosa's mother's medicine bag, on the counter since Part 1). Absence is worth more than a new presence: what Rosa came to pick up every night was left behind.
28. **Inside × outside must read in 1 s.** Whoever is in the street is an exterior shot (busstop) or an `outside` actor (clipped by the door glass and drawn behind whoever is inside; `outside: "dark"` = silhouette without a face). Never draw someone outside on the same wall, next to someone inside. A character's face is only revealed in the scene where the script reveals it.
29. **Distinct doors for distinct functions:** pharmacy = BACK (left, exit to the alley), STAIRS (middle, stairs to the pantry), street glass (right). All drawn in every pharmacy wide (`door`, `stairs`); alley: the same BACK, on the left.
30. **Low light darkens the whole set** (`lamp` state: only Sam's headlamp); the headlamp does not go off and come back without cause. Keys, jugs and bags have constant count and hand between cuts; the object that falls is the same one he picks up.
31. **Audit of the final MP4, frame by frame, per part, before delivering** (1 fps + the moments of the events + the last/first 3 s of the neighboring part): object that appears or vanishes, character that changes between cuts, geography, light, HUD. A review of the script and the still alone is not enough (the 12 script reviews of the redo did not catch what 4 video auditors found). Duration above 75 s is accepted.
