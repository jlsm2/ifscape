# Ifscape script rules (method, numbers, first second, text-only format)

Moved out of CLAUDE.md for context optimization. They hold as before; **read this whole file when writing or reviewing a script.** If it disagrees with CLAUDE.md, CLAUDE.md wins.

**Script method: specific, simple and viral (base: a study of viral "What if" videos, see `docs/viral-study.md`):**

- **Friend test:** every line is understood by a 12-year-old who hears it once, without pausing. No jargon ("syzygy", "obliquity", "barycenter"): use the everyday word ("the biggest tides", "Earth's tilt"). A technical term only if explained in the same sentence.
- **Specific, not generic:** the video has 2 or 3 "bar-table facts", one concrete number or detail the person repeats to someone else ("its light takes just over a second", "over 100 times brighter"). A line that would fit any topic ("the world would change forever") is cut.
- **One line, one idea:** at most ~12 words in the voice, always cause and then visible effect ("The Sun still pulls the sea. But only half as hard."). A definition never comes before the consequence.
- **Fact tied to an object:** every fact shows up on something visible on the stage (the stake, the shadow, the water) and touches the life of the viewer (night, home, food, coast, calendar).
- **Causal spine:** the thesis states the answer to the title; each jump follows from the previous one ("then", "now", "but") and is bigger than it; there are at least 3 short turns (up to 7 words); the question the audience would ask (e.g. "would we survive?") is answered inside the story, once, right afterwards.
- **Dry tone:** no emotion adjectives (terrifying, shocking, incredible), no exclamation marks, no "Did you know". The drama comes from image and sound. "Imagine" only in the opening.
- **Connect the lines (24/10/2026):** a fact followed by a fact, each with its own period, sounds like a list (moon-disappeared, scene 06: "...never come back. Storm floods get a little weaker." seemed to start something and stopped). When the 2nd sentence follows from the 1st, join them with a causal conjunction ("and", "so", "but", "then") in a single sentence (up to ~16 words) and open the next scene by picking up the previous one. Do not chain "and" everywhere: keep 3 or more short turns.
- **Fact told, not thrown (base: `docs/fact-presentation.md`, 14 videos):** a loose fact sounds like a list. Every fact sentence (number, mechanism or data point) must **set up** (before it there is the question, the problem or the yardstick it answers), **pay off** (the next sentence or the image in the same cut shows what changes because of it) and pass the **removal test** (delete the sentence: if the next one still makes sense alone, it was loose). Forms that work, pick one per fact:
  - **A. live quantity:** the number lives in the panel (`panel_rows`) and changes; the line only enters when it crosses a threshold everybody knows ("faster than sound").
  - **B. consequence, then the why:** "Hospitals collapse within hours." → "Nearly 90% of nurses were women."
  - **C. mechanism of up to 7 words and the effect right after:** "Every atom is now pure positive charge. And positive charges repel."
  - **D. chain link problem → solution → new problem,** with the connector in the sentence (but, so, and since, meaning).
  Complements: **one subject per scene** (the fact lines of a scene pull the same thread); **one beat per fact** (4 to 6 facts per video, each with its own line, cut or stamp, never two numbers in the same cut); the statistic goes in the panel or in the `fact` and the line carries the consequence as an everyday fraction ("9 in 10"); **setup and payoff** (the witness or a number from the first 25 s comes back in the last 15 s); doubt appears **once**, in its own sentence, followed by a firm fact: at most **1 per scene and 2 per video**, also counting "some say/think", "others disagree", "nobody can…", "odds are", "almost certainly". Before the draft, `.claude/skills/beat-map/SKILL.md` builds the map (question → fact → visible consequence → link).
- **Lessons from the viral study (apply to every new video; base: `docs/viral-study.md`):**
  - **Every narrator line names something that changes on screen.** Definition, mechanism and lists of exceptions go to the end card (`final_fact`) or are cut (protons, the worst viral, has 5 of 11 lines of definition; bacteria lost 20 retention points from 30 to 45 s and plants 9 from 24 to 33 s for the same reason).
  - **The witness has a small task that can fail** (grab, save, arrive, keep), shown with the threat by ~3 s, with a "near miss" in the middle. A cute cat does not become a formula (risk of "template" on YouTube); what gets copied is the task.
  - **Hook = title, number and image.** Deadline or live counter in frame 0; the first visible consequence enters by 1.5 to 3 s; the event comes at 6 to 10 s with a strong mark (flash, disappearance, color, falling object) and the sound peak in the same frame. The voice explains nothing before the event.
  - **One event that takes over the screen or reaches the lens at the climax** (water, dust, ice), with the mix peak in that frame.
  - **The panel number measures the DAMAGE** (or the distance to "you"), not where the camera is; the quantity changes per act and ends in a word ("HERE", "THE MOON IS GONE") when it makes sense.
  - **Signature number:** 1 per video, in 3 places (panel in frame 0, one sentence, post caption). At most **2 numbers in the voice** per video.
  - **Where the fact lives:** the statistic goes to the panel, sub-line or card; the voice says the consequence at human scale. The image comes before or together with the sentence, never after it has already announced it.
  - **Voice-free windows (decision B):** two of 2 to 4 s, one at the event and one at the climax; the video stays at 61 to 70 s and the budget drops to **~130 to 150 words on v4**.
  - **Final stretch:** false relief + short twist before "What should I imagine next?" ("everything returns" alone, at no cost, is the protons ending: 5.3% likes against 12.1% for electrons).
  - **Post caption:** 3 to 5 sentences that summarize the story with the signature number, then the question; no sources. **Test (decision C):** in 1 video, a "send this to someone who..." line only in the description; the spoken question does not change.
  - **Loop ending when cheap (decision D):** the last stage pose goes back to frame 0; the final question still comes before the last frame.
  - **Silence as a resource:** a short valley before the big twist and silence glued to zero.
  - **Panel that measures damage (decision A, test approved):** the big number is a `counter` in a metric unit that changes per act (distance to you, width, water height...), with `panel_clock: true` for the small, linear stopwatch under it, and `counter.final` ("HERE", "GONE") when the threat arrives. The stopwatch stays linear (owner rule). Test it with a `--dummy` render first. Before using it in a real video, look at the frame as a still.
  - **1-line end card (test approved):** `final_fact` (up to 16 words) with the real fact written during the pause before the final question. It is a fact, not a source; the line still says the sentence.
  - **Real fact inside the story (test authorized by the owner, in 1 video only):** the real data enters in the past tense inside the story ("9 in 10 of them were...") instead of only at the end; the `final_fact` stays. Mark the video as a test in `thesis.md`. In that video, `script-review` does not count the real data as breaking the "single premise"; `fact-check` checks the sentence as real data (group, place, year, edition). Compare retention and likes with the previous video before repeating.
  - **Really open the source:** do not confirm only through Wikipedia or a search summary; if the page blocks, open it in the owner's Chrome.
  - **Do not copy from the virals:** imperial units; gender or group-versus-group themes; statistics without an open source (a number that merely looks like a statistic); theatrical physics; orders with exclamation marks; a lesson sentence at the end; 113 to 210 s.
- **Tone regulator (oceans-no-salt):** "it does not have to be 100% true; moderate sensationalism makes the video pop. What it cannot do is say life would go on the same and show the man fishing for nothing." **Allowed:** strong tone, image and scale (murky sea, seagulls without food, a world in decline), a consequence said forcefully ("the chain comes apart from the bottom up"), a punchy line, a strong claim marked with "may/could/probably". **Not allowed:** a number or deadline without a source stated as fact ("in 7 days everyone starves"); an image that shows the world going on as normal when the premise is a calamity (a fisherman with a bite, clean blue water, a normal season); "the air runs out" without a study. The real-world fact (the former "In our world" line) is still checked as a real fact. In a calamity, the image is of a world in decline from the first jump.
- **The script shows the premise's world and the effect on HUMANS and on the PLANET (the owner: "this is the most important thing"):** before approving, check that there is at least one scene of gain or loss for the viewer (life scale: food, health, what changes day to day) and one of effect on the planet (sea, land, climate), each with a stated consequence and an image. If the research only finds a gain for humans, state the gain and leave the loss for the planet (the viruses framing: "small gain for you, big bill for the planet"). `script-review` asks this.
- **Lessons from ants-human-size (apply from the next video; details in local research notes):**
  - **Readable panel:** the big number is understood in 1 s without explanation (simple ratio, distance, count), never an index derived from our own calculation. 12-year-old friend test applied to the panel. Counter integer only; value ≤ 14 characters, label ≤ 26, `panel_rows` ≤ 2 with the title on screen.
  - **Opening scene = title only:** with `on_screen` the render shows only the title for the whole scene and ignores the `text`; the lines go in their own scene. Title in 2 lines of ~18 characters (the `\n` is ignored); short verb ("human-sized").
  - **A record ("biggest, first, only") requires checking the record in the source** (ants: "biggest ant that ever lived" was false, bigger fossils existed; the right comparison was "biggest living ant queen").
  - **Our own calculation becomes a labeled card ("a calculation") with a range** (e.g. k=100 and k=170) and the premise stated; never a firm fact. Unit abbreviations only from the channel standard (km, m, cm, kg); the rest spelled out ("newtons").
  - **Honest number on screen (information audit of ants; the lint enforces the first 2):**
    - **At most 3 pieces with a number per screen** (counter + 1 panel row + 1 card or line). The ants video had 5 in scene 02: 4,900,000x, x28,900, 9X, 33 TIMES and "50 times".
    - **The same number in one place only** (x28,900 in the panel and on the card at the same time does not inform).
    - **Number type in one of 3 forms**, always the same: "one study" (measured), "a calculation" (our own math, with a range) or "<year> estimate" (global estimate). No varying ("scaled estimate", "(a calculation)", "by one estimate") in the same video.
    - **The label says what was calculated, not what is assumed:** "LEG AREA x28,900", not "LEGS STRONGER BY x28,900", because the calculation is of area, not measured strength.
    - **Every number with a unit**, including the comparison one ("A human bites with 600 to 850" was left without "newtons").
    - **The math closes with what is on screen:** "33 TIMES" next to "about 5 centimeters" gives 34 for whoever checks. Either the screen shows 5.2 cm, or the card says 34.
    - **The word respects the source's metric:** if the source measures dry carbon biomass, the screen says "biomass" or "mass", not "weight".
    - **A debunked myth on the same screen, with a connector:** "You've heard 50 times. But one study measured about 9." The two numbers standing alone, with the same emphasis, look like a contradiction.
    - **The card and the panel row go away when the line changes subject;** they do not stay for three scenes on screen.
  - **Doubts:** `fact-check` decides the verb; `script-review` decides where they go (max. 2 per video); in a conflict, rephrase the sentence instead of gluing on "likely".
  - **Macro opening allowed** for "from small to human" themes: the camera starts at ground level and rises to eye-height POV; the closing repeats the same camera and the same light (setup and payoff).
  - **Set with layers and people** (park, plaza) in a scale theme; the set goes to the owner as a still before the heavy render.
- **No "nobody tested it" as a crutch:** when the research is thin, pick the strongest link with a source and use "may/could/would likely"; doubt counts once and short, never as the close nor as the central answer. If the source's model contradicts the exaggeration (e.g. Weitz 2015: transfer to higher trophic levels "nearly identical"), the sentence does not pass even with "likely".
- **Chain and witness task:** the beats follow the researched consequence CHAIN (each link with a source or marked as inference), and the witness task is something the premise really threatens (feeding the village, reaching the market, keeping the everyday network running), not a task that only fails slightly.
- **A calamity has to look like a calamity (oceans-no-salt; applies to the next videos):** in a collapse theme, the close states the global and irreversible effect with "would likely" when the research is an analogue ("Ocean life would likely collapse worldwide", "Species that vanish never return" + recovery time from a measured analogue), and the image is DRAMATIC and not static: visible signs of the decline at each jump (small fish floating belly up, birds giving up, water changing color, dropped objects), a slowly moving camera, and soft cuts (fade through black) at era jumps when they help. No gore close-ups or blood. The research first answers "what happens to humans and planet" (chain in parallel fronts); never "everything stays normal".
- **Countdown in a calamity:** when the theme is something that is going to happen (Moon disappears, Sun goes out, asteroid, gravity vanishes), the panel can open with a real countdown to the event, already running in frame 0, as in the virals ("ITS LIGHT ARRIVES IN 7:09", 17M). The number comes from a sourced fact (e.g. Moon light takes 1.28 s, Sun 8 min 20 s); if the real time is too short, count in a smaller unit (ms) or open at the instant of the event.
- **Retention (data from the first analytics):** voice and text tie on views; most people leave at 0:01 and those who stay watch ~23 s. Hence: a moving number and the strongest thing in the 1st second; a visible twist or jump every ~8 to 10 s; the best surprise at around 20 s; **duration between 61 and 70 s** (after analytics: 67 s = 15% completion, 72 s = 8.8%, 80 to 81 s = 5.7 to 8.9%; the 85 s ceiling, opened for the v4 voice, was revoked; plants-extinct, already recorded at 89.8 s, stays as is). **Word budget:** speech = words ÷ pace (v4 ~2.66 per second, v2 ~3.0) + 2 s final pause + ~0.45 s per scene; on v4, ~130 to 150 words with 2 voice-free windows of 2 to 4 s (`CLAUDE.md`; the ~145 to 165 were with 12 scenes and no windows), and ~160 to 185 on v2. Only applies when the owner asks for voice; the default is the text format. Count the words before sending to review: a script above budget is cut before the voice, not after.
- **Final call test:** in moon-disappeared the question "What should I imagine next?" becomes "Comment what I should imagine next." (script field `closing`, which keeps the 2 s pause), to measure whether it invites more comments. Compare comments per thousand views with the previous videos.
- **Voice v4, only when the owner asks for voice (default: text format, no narration):** `eleven_v4` accepts tone direction by tags in the text (`[calm, dry documentary narration]`; the tag is not spoken, v2 does not understand it and `voice.py` refuses) and closed sentences better in the Moon tests: the "and" fused scene 06 into one arc and the split final sentence ended in a conclusion tone. It is ~17% slower (Moon: 77.7 s against 66.5 s) and has no Style or Speed. Enable per script: `voice_model: "eleven_v4"`, `voice_direction` (all scenes), `direction` per scene (`""` turns it off) and `voice_stability` (default 0.5). The `.env` stays at `eleven_multilingual_v2` so old videos do not regenerate (the cache fingerprint includes model, direction and stability). The tag is billed (~30% more characters) and `voice` prints the billed characters per scene. The `.env` key only does TTS (401 on `/v1/models`). Numbers and units on v4 have not been validated yet: run `voice_check.py` and listen. The Moon and the published videos stay on v2. Tests and measurements in `build/moon-disappeared/voice_v4_test/`.
- **Critical lens:** before sending the script to review, reread every sentence with the 9 questions of the distrustful viewer (What? Why? So what? How do they know? Does it contradict? Exaggeration? Would they understand? Would they comment? Does it pass the platform?), plus the 10th, anti-slop (below). The full text of the 10 is in `.claude/agents/script-review.md`, the owner of the lens; `fact-check` applies only the source questions (How do they know? Exaggeration? Contradict?) and `director` only the physics of the image. Revised: the old "cycle until zero" process was revoked (it burned ~30 runs on plants-extinct without finding CRITICAL in the last rounds): reread the whole script once and pass each fix through the lens; `script-review` and `fact-check` run in 2 passes at most (the 2nd only on the changed lines) and stop with no CRITICAL or HIGH.
- **Sentence comes from the sheet:** the moon-disappeared errors were not search errors, they were writing errors (owl instead of rodent, bat without a source, forgotten why, "danger" and "kill" above the source). Every fact in `facts.md` has a sheet (literal excerpt, scope, why, allowed verb, forbidden words); each sentence is written from the sheet, and `script-review` checks line × sheet.
- **Natural cuts:** every cut has a reason tied to the line; in the same place and time, a continuity cut (the camera follows the movement, point of interest in the same region, ~30° of angle change); on a time or place jump, a transition of 4 to 8 frames tied to the whoosh (fade through black, light smear or camera that leaves and enters), never a hard cut between different lights (average brightness ≤ ~40 levels per frame); the sound crosses the cut. `director` writes the cut table in the plan; `animation-qa` and `sound-designer` check it. Full rules in `.claude/agents/director.md`.
- **Anti-slop:** the script cannot look like generic AI text, even if correct. Lens 10: "does it look like generic AI text?". Signs: number dump (max. 3 to 4 per video, each tied to a stage object), antithesis or effect triad ("The air holds. The food doesn't."), brochure words ("huge stock", "the real problem"), every scene in the label + fact mold, closing aphorism, encyclopedia-entry fact without a scene. Instead: second person with a concrete object ("Your charcoal still burns"), everyday comparison ("like climbing 100 meters up this hill"), varied rhythm, fewer facts better told. Test: would a good documentary narrator say this out loud? Full rules in the "Anti-slop" section of `.claude/agents/script-review.md`.
- **Single premise:** inside the story only the premise's world exists (Moon vanished = no eclipse, no "the Moon drifts away"). The real-world fact enters once, at the end, in the end card (`final_fact`) and in the closing line, without the phrase "In our world" (abolished: it was our own habit, none of the 15 virals of the study uses it; 9 of them put the real fact on a 1-line card). The separation comes from the card and from the image that returns to the real world (lesson from moon-disappeared).
- **Why and So what:** every big effect comes with the mechanism in one simple sentence ("Earth spins like a tilted top, and the Moon's pull kept it steady") and with what changes in the life of the viewer ("hotter summers and colder winters"). The question "would we survive?" is answered with the why.
- **Sourced deadline:** an intermediate stamp only exists if a study gives that deadline; otherwise the ladder skips (1 YEAR → MILLIONS OF YEARS).
- **Ending:** ends on a short impact sentence or on the real fact (`final_fact` card, without the phrase "In our world" (abolished)), a 2 s pause, and the call (`closing`; default "What should I imagine next?"). Without explaining the lesson.

Script rules:

- **First second (after the first TikTok analytics):** the Wi-Fi video lost most of the audience at 0:02; scene 01 had 2.2 s of silence with only the question over an almost still image.
  - In videos with narration, the voice starts at 0:00: scene 01 has `narration` with the question itself, besides the `on_screen`. No silence at the start.
  - In every video, the first second already shows movement and, preferably, the strongest thing in the video, while the question is on screen. Never a still image that looks like a cover.
- **Cover coming from the video itself:** the question stays highlighted on screen for a few seconds while the set moves, in a framing that works as a cover. Pick that frame as the cover in the app. The question must be on screen already in frame 0, because 3 of the 8 virals use the initial frame as the cover (`docs/covers.md`). The cover is frame 0 without the live panel (clean Blender frame + title + @): `render` writes `out/<slug>.cover.jpg` by itself.
- Lean mode (default): 5 to 6 scenes of a continuous story, with a visible twist every ~8 to 10 s inside the scene; short lines. (10 to 12 scenes with a change every 3 to 5 s was the old process, with `tools/blender/<slug>.py`.)
- One highlight word (`highlight`) per scene, preferably the word that carries the meaning.
- Every narrated scene carries `captions`: the narration hand-broken into chunks of 2 to 4 words that make sense alone (never separate article from noun, verb from short complement, or "Big" from "Bang"). Together, they repeat the narration word for word; `script.py` refuses if they do not match. The automatic break in `timing.py` is only a fallback.
- **Stamp and panel only with important information (default):** sourced deadline, date, "NOW" or a counter that measures damage. "SOON", "LATER" and any vague label do not go in: `stamp: null` and the panel disappears in the scene (the lint flags HIGH). The next scene without `stamp` inherits the previous one, so hide it on purpose.
- The time stamp only exists if the narration tells what happens at that instant ("One week later, the oceans…"). A general fact with no defined moment ("Today, Earth and Moon circle…") gets no stamp. This was asked for after the Moon video, which came out without stamps because the script explained in general instead of following a timeline.
- Total duration must exceed 60 s, because TikTok Creator Rewards only pays videos over 1 minute. `render` warns when it falls below.
- Every scientific claim goes into `sources` with a URL before publishing. If a number is not confirmed, the narration changes, not the other way around.
  - Sources are internal control: the owner does not post sources, neither in the description nor in a comment. Do not prepare source text for the post, nor remember to pin a sources comment.
- Speculative premises (π = 3) say that they are speculation.
- Narration for the American audience: metric units (meters, kilometers, centimeters, degrees), a decision for the whole channel (it used to be US units). The caption shows the same text as the narration, so the number form applies to the screen too. Number rule:
  - **Digits:**
    - quantities from 10 up, above all the numbers that impress and must be read at a glance ("13 million gallons", "500 years later", "10,000 years");
    - measured data with a unit, even below 10 ("almost 4 centimeters", "6 degrees");
    - years ("1964");
    - percentages with the symbol in on-screen text: "20-40%", "8%" (viruses-disappeared; before it was "60 percent"). In the spoken narration of a voiced video, the word stays.
  - **Spelled out:**
    - one to nine on their own in the sentence, not measured data ("two days later", "six months later");
    - expressions ("one by one", "one thing", "an hour later");
    - approximations and fractions ("a third of the way up", "about half", "millions of years"; measured data stays in digits: "almost 4 centimeters").
  - **Never:**
    - symbols °, $ (the % is allowed, see above);
    - unit abbreviations (mi, ft);
    - dates with slashes;
    - decimals. Round and say "about".

  `eleven_multilingual_v2` converts simple digits by itself in speech; the misreading risk is in the "Never" cases. On the first voice generation with a new number, listen to that scene. After every voice generation, run `py tools/qa/voice_check.py scripts/<slug>.json [--scenes 04,07]` (local faster-whisper): it transcribes and flags a swapped or swallowed word or a misread number; tone and emotion are still judged by ear.
- Title is only the question ("What if the Sun went out?"), without "Part 1".
- **Voice above the effects:** in videos with narration, the voice is never quieter than the sound effects. While someone speaks, the effects stay at least 8 LU below the voice; between sentences, no effect goes above the average speech volume. `sound-designer` measures this.
- **Text-only format (`"format": "text"`, channel DEFAULT):** no narration. The video already comes out with sound effects (`sfx.py`), with a continuous drone and ambience (never total silence), at -21 LUFS (at -16 the effects were too loud on Instagram). The 8 virals measured use "original sound", no platform music, and sit between -13 and -18 LUFS (`docs/sound-research.md`). Post without platform music; if you want music, leave it very low in the app.
  - Each scene has `text`: a list of short reading lines, 6 to 10 words, shown one at a time.
  - Screen time per line: 1 s + 0.3 s per word, minimum 2 s (`timing.line_seconds`).
  - The whole video has 15 to 25 lines, above 60 s.
  - One highlight word per line.
  - Screen line = consequence; the statistic goes to the panel (`panel_rows`) or to the `fact`; "Honest number on screen" (≤ 3 pieces with a number per screen; the lint enforces it).
  - Voice or text is asked of the owner in each video's briefing; do not alternate the format on your own. (The old rule of alternating between consecutive videos was replaced.)
