# Viral study synthesis

The 15 viral "What if" videos and three of our own videos (plants, bacteria, humans) were read second by second (local study notes hold the per-video analysis and evidence). Every rate here is correlation: the sample is small and some accounts use the same template. This file replaces the conclusions of the earlier narrative study where the two disagree.

**What does not separate high-C videos from low-C ones:**
- **Duration:** from 62 to 210 s.
- **Word density:** from 0.35 to 2.2 per second. The 4 most liked include 3 with 1.56 to 2.2 words/s.
- **Having a number moving:** protons has one the whole time and got 5.3%.

## 3. Us vs. them: the 5 differences that matter

1. **Definition line instead of consequence.** Bacteria lost 20 points from 30 to 45 s with three lines of chemistry over an empty pasture. In plants, the worst stretch was 24 to 33 s: empty sky and the oxygen explanation. It is the same defect as protons, the worst viral. In electrons, 7 of 11 lines are visible consequence.
2. **An event that takes over the whole screen:** it is in 5 of 5 continuous-take virals compared and in none of our 3 (water or glacier on the lens, whiteout, dive). Our sound peak does not fall on the biggest visual event.
3. **The witness looks, but wants nothing.** Doomsday (cat), meanwhile (baby) and rain (boat) have someone who can lose. Plants comes close ("your stall") and it is precisely our stretch of least loss (30 to 45 s: −9 points).
4. **We explain before showing.** Our voice starts between 0 and 2.3 s with context or exceptions ("but plankton..."). They hold title, image and number until ~5 s. Our biggest loss is from 0 to 3 s (plants −27 points, humans −19, bacteria −30), with the image still while the number falls.
5. **Static panel.** Plants left "1 week" fixed for 39 s and bacteria "6 months" for 14.6 s. In all the virals, the panel measures a live quantity or changes quantity on cue.

Word density is **not** on the list: meanwhile-men has 2.2 words/s and 20.3% likes. The problem is what the line says, not how many words it has.

What **not** to copy:
- imperial units;
- gender or group-versus-group themes, and social statistics without a source;
- theatrical physics (fact-check would reject pov-sea and moon2);
- orders with exclamation marks (with the Chris voice they sound like a trailer);
- cute animal as a formula (risk of "template" on YouTube);
- a lesson sentence at the end;
- 113 to 210 s;
- a central "+N" chip on every jump.

## 6. Decisions

**A. Panel: linear stopwatch or live quantity?**
None of the 15 virals uses only linear time. The options that worked are:
- the mirrored pair of 7692 (rotation + wind, with a fixed sum);
- the per-act quantity change of electrons (time → width of the Earth → "THE MOON IS GONE");
- the comparison label of ink ("AS COLD AS MARS");
- the unit-changing, accelerating time of rain.

Proposal: **the stopwatch stays small and linear, and the big number now measures damage in metric, changing quantity per act and ending in a word ("HERE" or a state).** Out: the source line under the number, which the owner rejected.
Cost: touching the overlays (`ifscape/`), some 2 to 3 h of code, the visual identity does not change. For time-jump themes, decide whether the stopwatch becomes an accelerating calendar, as in rain.

**B. Word density and voice-free windows**
Density does not explain likes (meanwhile-men has 2.2 words/s and 20.3%). What the virals have and we do not are **speech-free windows at the 2 biggest events** (P7).
Proposal: two windows of 2 to 4 s without voice, at the event (6 to 10 s) and at the climax. The 61 to 70 s stay the same and the target drops from ~145–165 to **~130–150 words on v4**.
Cost: low (pauses in the voice manifest and in the estimated duration).

**C. Final call**
"What should I imagine next?" already gives us comments at or above the virals' average. The 2 highest share rates came from "send this to…".
Proposal: test a send line **in the post description** in 1 video, without touching the spoken question. Cost: zero.

**D. Loop**
Closing on the same frame as the start is almost free in Blender (plants already does it by accident). The final question still comes before the last frame.
Proposal: adopt as the `stage` default. Cost: low.

## 7. Limits

- **Sample:** 15 virals, from several accounts with a repeated template, and 3 videos of ours.
- **Rates:** are from info.json on the download day.
- **Retention:** we only have a curve for our own videos, read at a few points.
- **Sound:** was inferred from volume, not listened to. The audio downloaded from pov-sea only goes to 38.9 s.
- **Facts in the virals:** the social statistics of the meanwhile videos were not checked.

## 8. Facts and script of the virals (supplement)

- Fact inventory of the 15 videos: where each fact lives (panel, sub-line, line, card, caption), how they were researched, the script template in slots and rewrites of our lines (local study notes).
- Check of 20 claims: score 5 right, 11 right but stretched, 2 wrong, 2 without a source. Typical error: the number matches the source and the sentence swaps what it measures (conflicts → wars, run-up → wave). It proposes 3 rules: literal verb and noun from the source; survey statistic with group, place, year and newest edition; formula number as a calculation with premises, said with "about" (local study notes).
- The same tests applied to the 8 published videos of OURS (local audit notes). Most frequent error: a deadline stamp without a source (4 of 8). The resulting rules are already in `script-rules.md`, `fact-check`, `script-review` and `lessons.md`.
